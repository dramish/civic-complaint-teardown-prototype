# Research: Method and Status

**Status: planned, not yet run.** No data has been collected. This folder holds the method, instruments and templates so the work can be reviewed and then executed. Findings will be added to `synthesis.md` only after they exist.

## Purpose

The teardown and PRD rest on a hypothesis: false "Resolved" closures are common enough to matter (PRD H1). This research gathers evidence for or against it, and checks whether the themes seen in app-store reviews hold up with people who have actually filed civic complaints.

## Two sources, kept separate

| Source | Type | What it can show | What it cannot show |
|---|---|---|---|
| Public app-store reviews | Secondary | Recurring complaint themes, in users' own words | Prevalence. Reviews skew toward very unhappy or very happy users |
| Short survey + a few follow-up calls | Primary | Whether the themes match real filing experiences; how people would respond to a verification step | Statistical significance at this sample size |

Findings from the two sources are reported separately and never combined into one number.

## Review coding plan

- Sample: about 100-150 Play Store and App Store reviews from the last 12 months, spread across star ratings (not only 1-star).
- Each review is tagged with one or more categories from `review-coding-sheet.csv`. The categories mirror the PRD's rejection taxonomy (PRD 6.2) so the two can be compared directly.
- Usernames are not recorded. Reviews are referenced by an internal row ID and a date.
- Report: counts per category, the sampling method, and the limitations above.

## Survey plan

- Audience: people who have filed a civic complaint (any app or channel). Reached through RWA groups, college and neighbourhood networks.
- Target: 10-15 responses and 3-5 short follow-up calls with respondents who agree.
- Questions: see `survey-form-questions.md`.
- Responses are anonymised before anything is committed here (no names, emails or phone numbers).

## Reporting rules

- State exactly what was done, e.g. "coded N reviews and surveyed M people". No rounding up.
- Say who the respondents were. Do not describe them as "Swachhata users" unless that was verified.
- Keep a short "what this did not tell us" section in the synthesis.
